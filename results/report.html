Mini GPT: results report

Six GPT-style language models written from scratch in PyTorch and trained on a single consumer GPU. The best one, model 6, writes short stories in simple English.

Verdict

  • Model 6 (TinyStories) works: correct grammar, a beginning, middle and end, dialogue, and it keeps track of who is who. It knows where a story ends.
  • Still weak at: small logic slips ("I will buy it for you" for a stone she found). Normal for 12M parameters.
  • What made the difference: data. 1 MB of Shakespeare gave made-up words; 2.2 GB of simple stories gave real English.
  • Honest score: test 1.364 vs val 1.366: it learned patterns, not stories.

3. Model 5: BPE on Shakespeare (a useful failure)

  • Same model as model 4, but reading 1,024 BPE word pieces instead of 65 characters.
  • Best val at step 750, then heavy overfitting: train 0.81 vs val 4.19 when early stopping ended it.
  • Per character it lost to model 4: test 1.628 vs 1.536. In pieces, 1 MB of text is only 411K training tokens, far too few for 11M parameters.
  • Lesson: word pieces need more data. With TinyStories (558M tokens), the same idea worked.

5. Setup

  • Model design (all GPTs): GPT-2 style: token + position embeddings, pre-LayerNorm blocks with multi-head causal attention and a GELU feed-forward, weight tying, AdamW with warm-up then cosine learning rate, gradient clip 1.0, best-checkpoint saving.
  • Models 1-5: Tiny Shakespeare (1.1M characters), split by position into 90% train / 5% val / 5% test.
  • Model 6: TinyStories V2 (2.7M stories, 2.2 GB). Train = the official train file; val and test = the two halves of the official validation file (0 exact duplicates with train). Our own byte-level BPE with 4,096 tokens, learned from a 40 MB train sample, 6 layers, 6 heads, width 384, context 256 tokens, dropout 0.0, 15,000 steps of batch 64.
  • Next: a grammar test to measure English across models, then a bigger model on real-world English (FineWeb-Edu).
